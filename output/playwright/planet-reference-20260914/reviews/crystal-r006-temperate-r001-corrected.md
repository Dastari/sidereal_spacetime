## Temperate r001: working visual gate remains open

Water, vegetation and elevated land communicate the family, but the reference composition is still not meaningfully reached. The result has a few immense almost flat green plates with straight, uniformly extruded grey cliff walls and a thin continuous cyan/beige border. Trees form rows along perimeters, while much of each interior is empty lawn. The reference has roughly six visible unequal elevated regions separated by bays and channels, irregular cliff buttresses and varied heights, denser forest clusters, and stronger shelf-to-deep-water variation. These differences remain obvious at approximately 300px body size.

A bounded composition trial should come before more small detail: reduce current landmass linear footprints to approximately .65–.75, increase/distribute groups enough to show about five to seven unequal regions per representative hemisphere while preserving intended total land coverage. This is a diagnostic starting range, not a fixed acceptance formula. Use one larger hero region, several medium regions, and a few subordinate fragments; avoid exchanging three equal plates for six equal plates. Break selected coast outlines into deep coves, peninsulas and lower shelves, with distinct cliff buttress height changes. The existing island kit can cover this if explicit larger continental variants are composed.

Place trees in a few unequal interior forest clusters with clearings and smaller transitional groves, not evenly spaced perimeter rows. Preserve readable open grass in selected places. After the macro trial, inspect water/shelf colors and cloud coverage against the same reference; they should support the land rather than obscure its outline. Tiny surface texture alone cannot close this composition gap.

## Crystal r006: working visual gate remains open

Crystal proportions and explicit light/dark facets are now credible; the largest heroes should not be enlarged globally again. Some local magenta influence is visible around the upper-left hero base in the first angle. Nevertheless, the second angle and reference-scale view put almost all large crystals at the limb, leaving the central face largely barren rock, tiny shards and a crater. The reference has a strong interior hero plus medium crystal clusters and a coherent luminous mineral fault among densely fractured rock. Current high, flat rock walls also compete with the crystal silhouette.

First make a bounded anchor/composition trial: move one or two existing hero/medium anchors into hemisphere interiors and add or redistribute several medium clusters that bridge the huge heroes and tiny chips. Retain quiet rock, but avoid an entire central hemisphere without a major crystalline feature. Do not camera-bake one perfect face: inspect the two standard angles and at least one further rotated hemisphere after redistribution. Lower selected over-tall bare rock walls by roughly half as a trial where their silhouettes compete with crystals; do not flatten the whole foundation.

Then refine the central rock into a few coherent fractured shelves and mineral seams, rather than an uninterrupted slab with scattered tiny chips. Keep luminous mineral influence spatially tied to fissures and hero bases. The light diagnostic shows that local influence can work; globally increasing magenta or brightening the entire crust would remove needed dark contrast. The final optical setup and its LOD/material/light budget remain separate technical decisions requiring evidence.
