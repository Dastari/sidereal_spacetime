# R006 native hull polymer study 01

Status: staged derivative; actual matched game image gate pending. No publication or final approval.

Owner requested a visible premium molded-plastic finish on the authorized R006 hull and floor r002. Root owns combined installation/publication. This study does not advance or alter the hull author's design revision, floor source, equipment or their approval records.

The preserved R006 source still has metallic .35 clean paint, .4 graphite seals and .2 service accents. Paint's roughness is texture driven. All 320 bevel modifiers in the aggregate source use one segment, most commonly 8 mm (138) and 5 mm (39). These chamfers catch light on a narrow planar face; they cannot produce a broad rounded highlight profile. Geometry remains approved and unchanged here. The floor already has a mapped polymer/fastener ORM, .08 clearcoat and real 4 mm two-segment perimeter bevel; its maps and geometry are preserved.

The candidate changes exactly named hull paint, nameplate, seal and service accent materials. Paint becomes metallic 0 with a .60 roughness-map multiplier, IOR 1.46 and .16 coat at .20 coat roughness; seals become matte dielectric .60; service accents use roughness .29 and .12 coat. Base colors, normal maps, metal hardware, glazing, emitters and equipment materials remain unchanged. Floor is not modified. The coat exceeds the reference's .08 starting point deliberately as a bounded highlight-response experiment; images, not parameters, determine acceptance.

`candidate.blend` retains the original editable meshes and modifiers. `geometry-audit.json` verifies exact geometry/transform/modifier digest equality. Seventeen staged GLBs retain byte-identical binary buffers, including geometry and textures. `study.json` records every source/candidate hash and material JSON change. Roughness is multiplied rather than replaced so authored variation remains available. Source nodes represent that same multiplication explicitly.

Current game lighting uses direct sun specular .3 and environment intensity .28. A matched baseline/candidate pair under those exact lights comes first; only then a diagnostic specular-only response adjustment, preserving light positions, diffuse energy, shadows and occlusion. No global ambient brightening or shadow disable is a proposed fix. The legacy side hull uses its existing exported finish; it must be assessed separately rather than accidentally editing shared equipment palette materials.

Embedded map audit: R006 clean hull/nameplate roughness G is uniformly 130/255 (.509804), yielding .305882 after the candidate multiplier. Its metallic B is uniformly 255, correctly multiplied to zero by the candidate factor. Floor r002 ORM is not constant: roughness G spans 84–117/255 with mean .3497; metallic B spans 0–191 for polymer/fastener regions. These data support keeping floor maps intact. Candidate export validation additionally compared all mesh/accessor/bufferView/node/image/texture/sampler descriptors and every non-target material record, with equality across all seventeen exports.

Actual source comparison: `blender-baseline.png` and `blender-candidate.png` use the same authored source camera and illumination, Cycles CPU 32 samples at 800×600. Both were visually inspected. The dielectric candidate remains only modestly different; these images do not establish premium-plastic acceptance. Narrow planar chamfers and broad flat panels remain prominent. Source render recipe and geometry audit script are retained. The initial denoise-enabled attempt failed because this installed Blender lacks OpenImageDenoise; its log is retained, and the successful pair disables denoising rather than substituting generated images. GPU game comparison is queued after the combined installation is ready.

## First actual game comparison

`runtime-baseline-bow.png` / `runtime-candidate-bow.png` and the matched deck pair use the combined installed R006 hull and r002 floor in actual `createWorld`, with no database connection or production override. Fifteen placed native export hashes matched the staged GLBs; both scenes contained 1,905 mesh resources and 443 native hull primitives. Camera equality and GL errors 0 were recorded in `capture.json`. The bow candidate has slightly brighter manufactured edges, with full-image mean absolute RGB changes of approximately 1.85/1.87/2.12 levels out of 255. That is a measured image difference, not a fidelity score. Visual inspection still finds the improvement too modest for premium-plastic acceptance. The unchanged deck pair is identical, as expected for a view dominated by the preserved floor and legacy side structures.

The new floor is visibly continuous. Existing sawtooth shadow bands on side trim, large triangular shadow patches on sloped armor/partitions, and mirrored room signs remain visible. They are separate presentation defects and are not claimed fixed by this material-only study.

The .65 `Light.specular` diagnostic is pixel-identical to the candidate and is not evidence that stronger PBR reflections cannot help. Installed Babylon 9.25 `ShadersInclude/lightFragment.js` uses `diffuse{X}.rgb` for directional PBR specular (line 141) and clearcoat (line 164); `vLightSpecular.a` supplies radius. The RGB `Light.specular` control is not the multiplier for this directional PBR path. The preceding .3-sun-specular explanation was therefore incorrect for native hull PBR, though it can affect other material/light paths. A next material-local trial will assert actual PBR `specularIntensity` and `environmentIntensity`, completed render IDs and light eligibility, preserving global diffuse lights and shadows.

Failed evidence is preserved: an initial hardcoded bow target captured only sky before the valid bounds-derived camera; its image is in `attempt-first-camera`. The initial game attempt also crossed a managed service restart and failed with connection refused. A sandbox-incompatible CLI helper was corrected before the accepted run. None of these failures are presented as asset defects or valid comparison frames. The accepted review browser was closed before other agents resumed GPU work.

## Material response and light-radius controls

`response-baseline-*` and `response-local165-*` are actual matched bow/whole views. Eighteen confirmed native paint materials changed to specularIntensity1.65/environmentIntensity1.65; before/after values, receiver light lists and completed render IDs are in `response-capture.json`. The result is somewhat brighter but does not produce a decisive premium-plastic appearance; no blanket response multiplier is recommended from this trial.

A separate `response-radius004-bow.png` restores both material intensities to1 and reduces only exterior-key radius .25→.04. Actual materials report physical falloff=true and directional vector length1.25. Installed Babylon adds radius/distance to direct-light roughness under this path: the old radius contributes .20 to roughness (paint .306→.506 and coat .20→.40). The smaller radius contributes .032 and visibly sharpens vent highlights. It does not by itself resolve the entire finish, and the change affects all receivers of that light, so production adoption remains a coordinated renderer decision. Render IDs advanced100→124 and GL errors were0.
