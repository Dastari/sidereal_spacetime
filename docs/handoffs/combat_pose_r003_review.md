## Diagnosis and recommended correction

| Owner defect | Concrete cause in the examined implementation | Required correction |
|---|---|---|
| Noncombat armed movement still turns slowly | `posePlacementHeading` snaps the outer presentation root to travel, but `createPoseState` still approaches noncombat facing at 160 degrees/second. `bodyFrame` immediately counter-rotates the snapped parent back toward that stale heading. | When aim is not active, align visual heading and yaw to current facing in the same frame. Low-ready arm interpolation must not reintroduce root turn inertia. Preserve bounded active-combat turning separately. |
| Rifle looks sideways/hip-carried | RIFLE and LONG_RIFLE specify `stanceYaw: 1.3` (about 75 degrees), with 65 percent sent to hips. The pocket offset and candidate corrections lower the stock. | Give ordinary rifles a modest, forward-facing stance, initially around 15–25 degrees total chest blade and 5–10 degrees hips. Fit the butt to the upper shoulder with a forward muzzle, then solve both arms. Preserve the previous broad, low stance as an explicitly named heavy-equipment profile; do not leave it as the ordinary-rifle default. These ranges are tuning seeds, not a measured accepted result. |
| No vertical cursor tracking | Normal gameplay supplies `pitch: 0`. The pointer helper intersects a fixed world-height plane (1.3 m above ship root), then only exposes yaw. Pitch samples exist but cannot be requested by the game. Weapon yaw also uses partial achieved torso yaw, so requested yaw can remain visually undershot even after root turn settles. | Derive a meaningful cursor target on actual visible geometry/deck, with a safe deck fallback. Resolve both yaw and pitch from the physical weapon pivot toward that target. Use the accepted planar yaw for authority, keeping local vertical presentation distinct. The solver should distribute requested aim across root, torso and arms rather than treating torso contribution as the weapon's entire achieved aim. Trace the beam only from the final solved muzzle, with obstacle clipping. |
| Combat mode lacks a cursor | The ordinary pointer does not visually encode active combat. | Show a small high-contrast combat reticle only while combat input is active and permitted over the world. Restore normal pointer over UI and on mode exit, seating, blur, hiding and teardown. The reticle is intent; the physical laser remains the actual obstruction/solver outcome. |
| No rifle low ready | Combat release drives the only pose weight to zero, then uses the hand's authored attachment. That is not a controlled two-hand low-ready pose. | Separate carried/low-ready acquisition from raised-combat acquisition. Maintain both grip contacts on a lowered rifle, with a downward barrel and relaxed shoulders, while suppressing laser/recoil outside combat. Preserve distinct seated/sprint release behavior. Avoid interpolating a rigid gun through torso geometry. |
| One-handed free hand is at chest | The shared `freeArmOffset` has Y=1.04 m and forward Z=-0.18 m. The free arm also reuses weapon orientation. | Target the free hand near the hip/thigh (initial Y about 0.75–0.82 m), with slight rearward offset and natural palm orientation. The exact position must account for equipped belt/holster geometry and both bodies. It should neither grasp an imaginary second grip nor track pistol pitch. |
| Knees bend sideways | Feet are captured after pelvis twist, but each knee pole uses canonical placement forward. The actual pelvis/foot forward can differ by nearly 50 degrees plus procedural heading. | Use the horizontal forward of each actual foot/final leg frame as the knee bend plane. Preserve stable foot targets before introducing a stance pelvis offset, or explicitly apply one coherent stance transform. Keep knee direction aligned with toe direction, and do not use a static actor-frame pole when the visible pelvis is rotated. |

The shared rig's head and helmet proxies are deliberately broad, and arm chains are short. Raising the rifle by a constant offset alone is likely to reintroduce reach or head/stock intersection. A better silhouette is not established by clearing these proxies; examine shoulders, palms, stock, sight line and knees in actual rendered geometry.

Preserving the heavy profile does not require changing the paired r002 sample JSON. An explicit profile may refer to the existing RIFLE authored sample set while carrying its own stance/placement parameters. Do not silently attach old stock coordinates to canonical, longer-stock geometry.
